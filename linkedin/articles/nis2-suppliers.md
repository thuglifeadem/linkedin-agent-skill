# NIS2 article (LinkedIn)

## Article

```
Your client falls under NIS2. In practice, so do you.

Picture a 15-person company in Limburg that has made parts for the same manufacturer for twelve years. Nobody there has ever heard of NIS2. Then a security questionnaire arrives from that client's procurement team. Thirty pages. Access controls, backups, incident response, patch management. Deadline: three weeks.

That company is not in scope of NIS2. It still has to answer.

Who NIS2 actually covers

On paper, the directive targets medium and large organisations, roughly 50 employees or €10M turnover and up, in sectors like energy, transport, banking, healthcare, digital infrastructure and manufacturing. If you run a small business, you could read the law and conclude it is not about you.

You would be technically right, and it would not help you much.

Why it reaches you anyway

NIS2 requires every regulated organisation to manage cyber risk across its supply chain, not only inside its own IT. Article 21 names supply chain security explicitly. So each essential or important entity now has to assess its suppliers: the subcontractor, the niche software vendor, the local manufacturer it has worked with for years.

For the supplier, that looks like a questionnaire. Sometimes 20 pages, sometimes 60. It asks you to describe, and often prove, things most small businesses have never written down.

The numbers behind the pressure

- Belgium put NIS2 into law on 18 October 2024. The registration deadline for most entities was 18 March 2025.
- By November 2025, about 4.000 organisations had registered with the Centre for Cybersecurity Belgium (CCB): roughly 1.500 essential and 2.500 important entities.
- Most of them buy from dozens of suppliers, often hundreds.
- Fines for essential entities go up to €10M or 2% of worldwide turnover, and up to €7M or 1,4% for important entities. That is why they push the question down the chain.

What it costs a small supplier

A questionnaire like this is rarely hard because of the questions. It is hard because the answers are scattered. The backup policy lives in the head of your IT partner. The access rights were set up by someone who left two years ago. The incident procedure was never written down. Collecting all of that, writing it up and getting it approved easily takes several working days.

And it rarely stays at one. Every large client uses its own format, so the same questions come back a few months later in a different order. For a small company, that is time taken straight from paying work.

And since 2 August 2026, the AI Act's transparency rules apply as well. If you run a chatbot for customers or publish AI-generated content, you have to say so. Expect that question to show up on the next questionnaire too.

What to do this quarter

If you supply larger clients in manufacturing, energy, healthcare, logistics, financial services or digital services, the useful question is not "does NIS2 apply to me". It is these four:

1. Have I received a questionnaire yet? If not, when will the first one arrive?
2. Where does my evidence live? Policies, access logs, backup records. Or is it spread across someone's inbox and a shared drive?
3. Who in my company owns this?
4. Which level of the CCB's CyberFundamentals framework fits my company? It is free, it is Belgian, and many clients accept it as a common language. Start with the Small or Basic level.

Most small businesses I talk to have no good answer to the first three. That is not a criticism. Compliance tools were built for enterprises with a security team, not for a 15-person company trying to keep up with a client's procurement department.

I see both sides of this. I spent the last years inside a grid operator and three banks, the kind of organisations that send these questionnaires, and at FidelSec I now help companies get ready for NIS2 and ISO 27001. At AGenius we have spent the past months working out how a small supplier can answer these questions without a security team of its own.

So I am curious: if you are a supplier, how many of these questionnaires did you get this year, and how long did the last one take you?

```
